## What drives the price up or down?

The single biggest factor is response time: a pipe found and shut off within an hour or two costs far less to dry out than one that ran all night while the house was empty.

Other factors that move the number:

- **How contaminated the water is.** A clean supply-line break is Category 1 water under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the easiest and cheapest category to dry. If that water sat long enough to pick up contaminants from building materials, or a backed-up fixture was involved, the category (and the cost) goes up.
- **How much material has to come out.** Wet drywall, baseboard, and insulation that can't be dried in place get cut out and replaced. A plaster wall in an older Whittier or All Saints bungalow often means more demo than a newer build in Prairie Green.
- **Square footage and number of rooms.** Drying equipment (air movers, LGR dehumidifiers) is priced per affected room, not per house.
- **Whether the pipe froze in multiple spots.** A single hard freeze can split a line in two or three places along its run, especially in an uninsulated crawl space or an exterior wall, meaning more than one repair point.
- **Code upgrades.** If the original plumbing run doesn't meet current code, the plumber may need to bring that section up to code while making the repair, which adds material and labor cost.

## Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe in Sioux Falls?

Most standard homeowners policies cover a burst pipe because it's sudden, accidental water damage from inside the home, but insurers can deny the claim if the house was left without heat and the pipe froze as a direct result of that neglect.

Insurers expect a homeowner to maintain reasonable heat in the house, even when traveling. If an adjuster determines the freeze happened because the furnace was off or the thermostat was set unusually low for an extended period, they may treat it as a maintenance failure rather than a sudden loss. Keep the thermostat no lower than the mid-50s when you're away during a Sioux Falls winter, and know where your main shutoff valve is.

Document everything before cleanup starts: photos of the break, photos of standing water, and a note of when you found it. That documentation protects the claim regardless of which category of loss it turns out to be. For the fuller picture on what a standard policy typically pays and what it typically excludes, see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) has a plain-English breakdown of what a standard policy covers.

## What happens during a burst pipe repair job?

A burst pipe job runs in three phases, and most plumbers only do the first one.

1. **Stop the water and fix the pipe.** A plumber shuts off the supply, cuts out the damaged section, and replaces it. If the break is behind a wall, this usually means a small access cut rather than opening the whole wall.
2. **Extract standing water and dry the structure.** A [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) crew uses extraction equipment to pull standing water, then sets air movers and dehumidifiers and checks moisture levels daily with a meter until the wood, drywall, and subfloor read dry to the IICRC S500 standard. This typically takes three to five days depending on how much material got wet.
3. **Rebuild what came out.** Wet drywall, insulation, baseboard, and sometimes flooring get replaced. This is where a lot of homeowners get stuck juggling a separate contractor, which is the main reason this part of the bill often costs more than it needs to: [burst pipe repair](/services/burst-pipe-repair/), the drying, and the [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) can all be handled by one crew instead of three separate companies passing the job back and forth.

## How do you know if your pipes burst from freezing?

The clearest sign is no water pressure at a faucet that worked fine the day before, paired with a wet spot, stain, or sagging section on a nearby wall or ceiling.

Other signs: a hissing or dripping sound from inside a wall, frost visible on an exposed pipe in a crawl space or garage, or a sudden spike on the water meter if the break hasn't been found yet. Pipes running along exterior walls, in unheated crawl spaces, or in a garage are the most common failure points in Sioux Falls homes during a hard freeze.

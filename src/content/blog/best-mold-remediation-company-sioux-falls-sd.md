## What Should You Check Before Hiring a Mold Remediation Company?

A trustworthy mold remediation company in Sioux Falls should be able to answer six questions clearly, before you sign anything. Ask about technician certification, whether they write a scope of work before starting, how they contain the work area, whether clearance testing is done by someone other than the remediation crew, whether they fix the moisture source (not just the mold), and whether they can handle the rebuild afterward.

These aren't arbitrary checkboxes. Each one maps to a real failure mode in this industry: uncertified crews that disturb mold instead of containing it, verbal estimates that balloon mid-job, and remediation that gets marked "complete" by the same company that did the work, with no independent check.

## Why Do Certifications Matter More in South Dakota Than Other States?

Certifications matter more here because South Dakota has no state licensing board for mold remediation, so a credential like IICRC certification is one of the only objective signals a homeowner can verify. The [Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification](https://www.iicrc.org/) trains and certifies technicians against the ANSI/[IICRC S520 standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/) for professional mold remediation, which covers containment, PPE, and post-remediation verification.

When you call a company, ask directly whether the technician coming to your house holds the Applied Microbial Remediation Technician (AMRT) credential specifically, not just a general IICRC membership. A company that answers that question without hesitation has usually invested in training beyond the minimum.

## What Does Post-Remediation Clearance Testing Involve, and Why Should You Ask About It?

Post-remediation clearance testing is an independent check, done after remediation work is finished, to confirm the mold problem was actually resolved. The [EPA's mold cleanup guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) recommends visual inspection and, in many cases, air or surface sampling to verify conditions before an area is considered clear.

The key word is independent. If the same company that removed the mold is also the one declaring the job clean, that's a conflict of interest worth asking about directly. Ask whether clearance sampling is handled by a third-party industrial hygienist or a separate inspection arm of the company, and ask to see the lab results, not just a verbal "it's good now."

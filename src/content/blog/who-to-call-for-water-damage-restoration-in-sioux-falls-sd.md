## Who Should You Call First for Water Damage?

The first call depends on what is still happening. If water is actively flowing, you need to stop the source before calling a restoration company. Here is the right call sequence:

**1. Stop the water first.**
If a pipe burst or an appliance line failed, shut off the main water supply to your home. The shutoff is usually near your water meter or in the utility room. If you cannot find it or cannot stop the flow, call a licensed plumber.

**2. If there is an electrical or gas hazard, call 911 or your utility.**
Standing water near an electrical panel, outlets, or appliances is a safety emergency. Do not enter the area. Call 911 or Xcel Energy / your local utility before anything else.

**3. Call Crew Restoration & Construction at (605) 965-2727.**
Once the source is controlled and the space is safe to enter, call Crew. They will ask you a few quick questions about the loss, dispatch a crew, and begin extraction and drying. They also help you document the damage for your insurance claim from the moment they arrive.

**4. Call your insurance company after mitigation starts, not before.**
This surprises many homeowners. You are not required to get insurer approval before starting emergency mitigation. In fact, most policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Start drying, then notify your carrier. Crew handles the documentation that protects your claim.

Once a crew is dispatched, here is what the first visit looks like:

**Moisture mapping.** The crew uses thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find water behind walls, under flooring, and in ceiling cavities. Water travels further than it looks. A wet spot on your kitchen floor in the All Saints neighborhood might mean moisture has wicked into the subfloor and wall cavity.

**Water category assessment.** Not all water is the same. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line. Category 2 (gray water) comes from appliances or dishwashers. Category 3 (black water) includes sewage or floodwater and requires full PPE and sanitization. The category determines how aggressively materials need to be removed versus dried in place.

**Extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull standing water out fast. The sooner water is out of carpet, flooring, and wall cavities, the less secondary damage occurs.

**Drying equipment placement.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned based on the moisture map, not just where water is visible. Equipment runs continuously, typically for 3-5 days, until moisture readings reach the IICRC-defined drying goals for each material type.

**Daily monitoring.** A technician checks moisture readings each day and adjusts equipment. This daily log becomes part of your claim file.

**Scope of work documentation.** Before any demolition or material removal, Crew provides a written scope. Nothing is torn out without your sign-off.

## What About the Insurance Claim?

Most standard homeowners policies in South Dakota cover sudden, accidental water damage from internal sources, such as a burst pipe or a failed water heater. They typically do not cover gradual leaks or flooding from outside (that requires a separate flood policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance)).

Crew Restoration & Construction works directly with insurance carriers and adjusters. Their documentation package includes:

- Moisture readings with timestamps and room diagrams
- Photographs taken before, during, and after each phase
- Equipment logs showing what ran, where, and for how long
- A written scope of work tied to Xactimate line items (the estimating platform most adjusters use)

This paperwork matters. A claim without proper documentation is a claim that gets underpaid or disputed. Having a restoration company that knows how to build that file is one of the most practical reasons to call a certified company rather than a general handyman.

## How does ServPro's biohazard cleanup pricing typically work?

ServPro is a national franchise brand, and each location is independently owned and operated, so pricing structure and rates are set locally rather than by one national price list. Most ServPro franchises, like most restoration companies working trauma or biohazard scenes, bill on an hourly labor rate per technician plus the cost of materials, PPE, and biohazard waste disposal, added up after the work is scoped or completed.

That model isn't unusual in this industry. The practical issue for a homeowner or property manager is that an hourly-plus-materials estimate is harder to pin down up front than a single number, and which franchise picks up your call can affect response time and who actually shows up.

## How does a locally owned Sioux Falls company price it differently?

A locally owned crew can walk the affected room, assess contamination category and square footage, and hand you one written scope and price before any cleaning starts. That's the core difference: a defined number set after an in-person assessment, instead of an hourly meter that runs until the job is done.

This matters most when insurance is involved. An itemized, pre-agreed scope is easier for an adjuster to review line by line than a running labor invoice, and it gives the property owner a clear ceiling going into the job.

## What actually drives the total cost either way?

The price moves on the same handful of factors no matter who's doing the work: square footage affected, whether porous materials like carpet, subfloor, or drywall have to be removed and replaced, the contamination level present, and how much PPE and biohazard waste disposal the job requires under [OSHA's bloodborne pathogens standard](https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens).

Most biohazard cleanup jobs in the Sioux Falls area fall somewhere between a few hundred dollars for a small, contained incident and several thousand for a job requiring material removal, odor treatment, and disposal of regulated waste. Those are typical industry ranges, not a quote: every scene is different, and a legitimate company documents the actual scope before charging for it. Work should follow the [ANSI/IICRC S540 standard for trauma and crime scene cleanup](https://iicrc.org/s540/), regardless of which company you hire.

## Who handles the insurance paperwork?

This is often the deciding factor for families dealing with a loss. Homeowners and renters policies sometimes cover biohazard cleanup tied to a covered incident, but coverage depends on the specific policy and cause, so it's worth confirming with your carrier before assuming it's included.

What a company can control is how clearly it documents the job for your adjuster: photos, a written scope, itemized disposal records. Ask any company you're considering, ServPro franchise or otherwise, who on their team handles that communication and whether they'll work directly with your adjuster.

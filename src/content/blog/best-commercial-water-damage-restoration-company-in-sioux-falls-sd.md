## What should a commercial water damage restoration company in Sioux Falls actually offer?

A commercial-grade contractor should offer 24/7 dispatch, technicians trained to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), equipment sized for large square footage, and a single point of contact who manages both the drying scope and the insurance paperwork. Residential crews can be excellent at a flooded basement and still lack the truck-mounted capacity, desiccant dehumidification, or staffing depth a 10,000-square-foot office suite or a multi-unit commercial building needs.

The gap usually shows up fast: a residential-scale setup under-dries a large open floor plan, moisture lingers in wall cavities and subfloor, and the business reopens before the structure is actually dry, which is exactly the condition mold needs. The [EPA notes mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) on wet materials, so under-equipped drying doesn't just cost time twice. It risks a second loss.

## What certifications and licensing should you verify before signing a work order?

Verify the contractor holds an active South Dakota contractor license and that its technicians carry [IICRC certification](https://www.iicrc.org/), not just a company-wide claim of being "certified." A commercial work order is a meaningful contract, and a business owner is entitled to ask for the license number and insurance documentation before a crew starts tearing out drywall.

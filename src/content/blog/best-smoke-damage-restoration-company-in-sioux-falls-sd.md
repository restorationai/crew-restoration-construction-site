## What IICRC certification actually means for a smoke damage job

IICRC certification means technicians were trained and tested against the [Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification's](https://www.iicrc.org/) standards for fire and smoke restoration, not just "restoration experience" in general. Ask specifically whether the company follows the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/), which governs how soot is classified, how surfaces are tested, and how a job is documented from start to finish.

This matters because smoke damage isn't uniform. Protein smoke from a kitchen fire behaves differently than synthetic smoke from a burned mattress or electrical fire, and each calls for a different cleaning agent and sequence. A company working from the S700 standard will classify the type of residue before they start wiping anything down, which protects your walls, cabinetry, and contents from the wrong chemical on the wrong surface.

## How do you know a company can remove the odor, not just cover it up

Real odor removal means the source of the smell, the soot particles embedded in porous materials, gets cleaned, sealed, or removed, not sprayed over with air freshener. A company that only fogs a space with scent and calls it done hasn't actually solved the problem; the odor tends to return within days, especially in humid weather.

Ask what equipment they use: thermal fogging to neutralize odor molecules at the source, HEPA air scrubbers to pull soot particulate out of the air, and in some cases hydroxyl or ozone generators for unoccupied spaces. Porous materials that can't be fully cleaned, like heavily charred carpet pad or insulation, usually get removed rather than deodorized around. Our [guide to how professionals remove smoke odor](/blog/smoke-odor-removal-techniques/) walks through why DIY attempts with vinegar and baking soda rarely solve a whole-house smoke problem.

## Does the company handle contents cleaning and pack-out, or do you need a second vendor

The strongest companies handle contents cleaning and pack-out themselves instead of handing you off to a separate vendor mid-claim. That means an inventory of smoke-damaged belongings, ultrasonic or manual cleaning for salvageable items, and secure off-site storage while the structure dries and rebuilds.

This is one of the most overlooked parts of a smoke damage claim. Furniture, electronics, clothing, and keepsakes in a home near All Saints or Whittier often carry more sentimental and financial value than the drywall around them. A company that offers [contents restoration and storage](/services/contents-restoration/) under the same contract keeps your claim organized and your belongings accounted for, instead of juggling receipts from two or three different companies.

## Who handles the insurance documentation for a smoke damage claim

The restoration company should document the loss for your insurer, not leave that work to you. That means photographing every affected room before cleanup starts, writing a detailed scope with the extent of soot and odor damage, and keeping that paper trail consistent with what the adjuster sees on-site.

A company used to working directly with South Dakota insurers knows what adjusters expect to see: room-by-room photos, a written scope broken out by task, and receipts for anything disposed of rather than cleaned. That documentation is what gets a claim approved without a second visit or a dispute over what was actually damaged.

## Should the same contractor handle cleanup and reconstruction

Handling cleanup and reconstruction under one contractor means one point of accountability for the whole job, from the first soot wipe-down to the last coat of paint. You're not managing a handoff between a cleanup crew and a separate general contractor, and nothing falls through the cracks between the two scopes.

Fire and smoke jobs often require more than cleaning: scorched drywall, damaged cabinetry, or a section of subfloor may need to be removed and rebuilt.

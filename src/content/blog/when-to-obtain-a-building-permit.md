**TL;DR:** Building permits are typically required for structural or system-level work: additions and remodels, finished lower levels, decks, re-shingling or re-siding, new windows or exterior doors, and storage sheds. You generally don't need one for cosmetic work like painting, wallpaper, cabinets, or retaining walls under 4 feet. Requirements vary by location and project, so when in doubt, call the City of Sioux Falls before you start.

Say you're ready to finish the basement before winter, or you're replacing the aging roof on your Sioux Falls home. Before you schedule a single subcontractor, you need to know whether the city requires a permit, because getting this wrong doesn't just risk a fine. It can mean tearing out finished work to let an inspector see what's behind the drywall.

## Which home projects actually need a building permit in Sioux Falls?

Requirements vary based on your location and the project, but work that changes a home's structure, electrical, plumbing, or mechanical systems generally needs a permit. This covers most of the work homeowners think of as "real" renovations, not just cosmetic updates.

Projects that typically require a permit include:

- Building a storage shed
- Finishing a basement or lower level
- Adding or replacing a deck
- Re-shingling a roof or re-siding a house
- Razing or demolishing a structure
- Additions and remodels
- Replacing windows or exterior doors
- Fences, carports, home occupations, signs, and driveways, which need permits from the Zoning Department

Electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work each need their own separate permit, even when they're part of a bigger remodel. ## Which projects don't need a permit?

Cosmetic and low-risk work generally doesn't require a permit, because it doesn't affect a structure's safety or code compliance. Swing sets and playground equipment, kitchen cabinet installs, retaining walls under four feet, and finish work like painting or wallpaper typically fall into this category.

If your retaining wall is close to that four-foot line, it's worth a quick call rather than a guess.

## What happens if I skip the permit and get caught?

Skipping a required permit can force you to undo finished work so an inspector can see what's underneath. If you frame and drywall a wall without the city signing off first, the inspector may require you to remove the drywall, and sometimes the ceiling cover, to verify the framing, wiring, and plumbing were done to code.

That's not a technicality designed to annoy homeowners. A permit and its inspections exist to confirm structural integrity and basic life safety for the building, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical systems.

## Who actually pulls the permit, me or my contractor?

Either you or your contractor can pull the permit, but it depends on who's doing the work. If you're tackling the project yourself, you go to the city and pay for the permit directly, and you'll need separate homeowner permits for any electrical, plumbing, or mechanical portions. When you hire a licensed subcontractor for those trades, they typically pull their own permits under their license.

If you're ever unsure whether a project needs a permit, the safest move is to call the city and ask before work starts, not after.

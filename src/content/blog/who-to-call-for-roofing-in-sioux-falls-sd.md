## Who do you call first after storm damage to your roof?

Call a roofing contractor first when water is actively getting in, shingles are visibly missing, or a tree limb has punched through the deck. A roofer or restoration crew can tarp the opening, stop interior damage from spreading, and start the documentation your claim will need.

Call 911 or your utility company first only if there's a downed power line touching the house, a gas smell near the roofline, or a section of roof structure that looks like it could collapse. Those are safety calls, not repair calls.

Hold off on calling your insurance company until after the roof is tarped and photographed. Filing the claim first, before anyone has looked at the damage, usually just means more back-and-forth later. Homeowners also have a general duty to prevent further damage once a loss happens, which a quick tarp satisfies. ## How can you tell it's storm damage and not normal wear?

Storm damage usually shows up suddenly and matches a specific weather event: a hailstorm, high wind, or a falling branch. Wear and tear shows up gradually over years and tends to be denied by insurers as a maintenance issue rather than a covered loss.

Look for round or irregular dents in shingles (hail), bruised or missing granules in the gutter runoff, shingles that are torn or lifted in one direction consistent with wind, or a fresh leak that started right after a storm rolled through. Prairie weather around the Sioux Empire brings hail and straight-line wind events that can hit roofs hard with little warning, especially on homes in older neighborhoods like McKennan Park or All Saints where roofing materials have had more years of UV and freeze-thaw exposure.

If you're not sure which category your roof falls into, a [roofing](/services/roofing/) inspection can tell you quickly, and it's worth getting that answer before you talk to your insurer.

## What happens when you call for a roof damage assessment in Sioux Falls?

On the first call, a dispatcher will ask for your address, a description of the damage, and whether water is actively entering the home. Have that ready along with any photos you were able to take safely from the ground.

Once a crew arrives, they inspect the roof deck and shingles, check the attic for moisture intrusion, and photograph everything before touching anything, since that documentation is what supports an insurance claim later. If the roof is actively leaking, the priority is a tarp or temporary patch to stop water intrusion, not a full repair on day one.

After the inspection, you should get a written scope describing the damage and the recommended repair or replacement, not just a verbal estimate. That document is also what most insurance adjusters want to see before they'll move a claim forward.

## When should you call your insurance company?

Call your insurer once you have photos and a written scope from the roof inspection, not before. Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental roof damage from wind or hail; they generally do not cover damage from age, neglect, or gradual wear, so documentation of the storm event matters. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance) has a plain-language breakdown of what typically is and isn't covered under a standard policy.

## Restoration emergencies common in Dakota City

Dakota City sits low along the Missouri River bottomlands, and that geography drives most of the restoration calls in the area. River-adjacent soil holds moisture longer than upland ground, so basements and crawlspaces here are more prone to seepage after heavy spring rain or rapid snowmelt than homes built on higher terrain. Nebraska's freeze-thaw winters also stress older water lines and roof flashing, and a cracked supply line or ice-damaged shingle can go unnoticed for days in a part-time or rural property before it surfaces as a stained ceiling or a soft spot in the subfloor. Add in Midwest thunderstorm season, with the wind and hail that come through the river valley most summers, and you get a steady mix of water intrusion, roof damage, and the mold growth that follows when a leak sits too long.

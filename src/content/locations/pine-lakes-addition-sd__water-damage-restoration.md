## Why Pine Lakes Addition Properties See Water Damage Issues

Pine Lakes Addition sits in the growth corridor around Sioux Falls, where "addition" subdivisions have gone in fast over the last few decades on clay-heavy prairie soil. That soil doesn't drain quickly, and after a heavy spring rain or rapid snowmelt, groundwater can sit against foundation walls longer than homeowners expect. Combine that with the hard winters typical of this part of South Dakota, and you get a second recurring cause: supply lines and exterior spigots that freeze and split when a cold snap hits an uninsulated crawlspace or an exterior wall cavity. Both patterns tend to produce the same result, water finding the lowest point in a basement or utility room before anyone notices a problem upstairs.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Pine Lakes Addition

We start by locating the source and shutting it down, then move straight into extraction so standing water isn't sitting on subfloor or carpet pad any longer than it has to. From there we map moisture in the walls, flooring, and framing with meters rather than guesswork, because drywall can look dry on the surface while the stud cavity behind it is still saturated. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and adjusted based on daily moisture readings rather than left running on a fixed schedule. For newer construction common to this part of Sioux Falls, that usually means engineered wood subfloor and OSB sheathing, both of which hold moisture differently than older solid lumber and need closer monitoring to avoid secondary swelling or delamination.

## Local note

Many homes in Pine Lakes Addition, like other recently platted additions around Sioux Falls, rely on sump pumps to manage basement groundwater rather than gravity drainage alone. A sump pump without battery backup can fail within minutes of a power outage during a summer storm, and because these systems often sit in a finished or semi-finished basement corner, the first sign of trouble is usually a damp baseboard or a musty smell near the lowest point of the room rather than visible standing water. If your home has a sump system, it's worth confirming the backup power source works before storm season, not after.

## Why Rowena Properties See Water Damage Issues

Rowena sits in flat, clay-heavy farmland typical of southeastern South Dakota, and that soil doesn't drain quickly. When spring snowmelt or a heavy summer storm saturates the ground around a foundation, water has nowhere to go but in, especially through older poured-concrete or block foundations that were never fully waterproofed. Many homes in and around Rowena rely on private wells and septic systems rather than municipal lines, which means a failed sump pump or a cracked pressure tank can flood a crawlspace for days before anyone notices. Winters here bring hard freeze-thaw cycles, and that stress is a common cause of burst supply lines in exterior walls and unheated additions, something we see repeatedly once the January cold snap breaks.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rowena

The first step on any call is confirming the water source and shutting it off, whether that's a municipal shutoff, a private well pump, or a frozen line that's finally let go. From there we extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps, pull back carpet and pad where needed, and start air movers and dehumidifiers to begin structural drying. In older Rowena homes with plaster or full dimensional lumber framing, that drying phase typically runs longer than it would in a newer drywall-and-OSB build, because denser, older materials hold moisture and release it slowly. We track moisture content daily with meters rather than guessing by feel, and we don't call a job finished until readings are back to a dry standard, not just "dry to the touch."

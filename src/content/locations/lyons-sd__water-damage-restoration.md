## Why Lyons Properties See Water Damage Issues

Lyons sits in a part of South Dakota where winter temperature swings are abrupt and the ground itself works against older plumbing. Pipes run through unheated crawlspaces or shallow basements in many of the area's older farmhouses, and a hard freeze followed by a quick thaw is a common way a supply line splits open overnight. Heavy clay soil common across this region also drains slowly, so a spring melt or a few inches of rain can sit against a foundation for days, pushing moisture through block walls and old mortar joints long after the storm has passed. Homes on private wells and septic systems, which are typical in and around Lyons, add another layer: a failed sump pump or a backed-up line does not get caught by a municipal alert system, so damage can go unnoticed until flooring starts to cup or a musty smell shows up in a closet.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lyons

The first step on any call is confirming what kind of water we are dealing with and where it has traveled, not just where it is visible. In rural and older properties around Lyons, that means checking under subflooring and inside wall cavities that may have absorbed water from a slow leak rather than a dramatic burst pipe. We pull standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment depending on access, then set containment and drying equipment based on the materials involved. Older plaster and real wood subfloor, both common in this area's housing stock, hold moisture longer than drywall and engineered flooring, so we monitor with moisture meters rather than guessing at a drying timeline. Structural drying is tracked daily and documented against the IICRC S500 standard so the drying log holds up if an insurance adjuster asks for it later.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Water Damage

Extraction equipment, air movers, and low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers make up the core of most jobs, but the mix depends on what the structure is made of. A home with a poured concrete basement dries differently than one with a dirt-floor crawlspace, and we adjust equipment placement and drying time accordingly. Where contamination is a concern, such as water that has come into contact with soil or backed-up plumbing, we treat affected materials according to category guidelines before drying begins rather than drying first and testing later.

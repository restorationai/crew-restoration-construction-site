## Why Brookings Properties See Reconstruction Needs

Eastern South Dakota's winters are hard on structures. Repeated freeze-thaw cycles stress foundations and slab joints, and heavy snow loads have been known to strain older roof framing, especially on homes built before current snow-load codes were adopted. Brookings also sits along the Big Sioux River watershed, so spring thaw and heavy rain events can push groundwater into basements near low-lying streets, which turns a water intrusion into a full subfloor and framing rebuild if it isn't caught early. Add in the churn of student rentals near campus, where properties change tenants every August, and landlords often discover damage from a slow leak or a space heater fire right when they need the unit back online for the next lease cycle.

## Our Reconstruction Services Process in Brookings

Once mitigation crews have removed damaged materials and verified the structure is dry, our reconstruction team documents the scope with photos and measurements for the adjuster, then sequences the rebuild: framing repairs first, followed by insulation, drywall, and trim, with flooring and paint last. On older downtown homes we pay close attention to matching plaster transitions and original trim profiles where the owner wants to preserve character rather than switch everything to modern drywall. On newer builds near Pheasant Ridge, the work tends to move faster because materials and assemblies are more standardized. Brookings County issues building permits for structural work, and we pull those permits and schedule inspections as part of the job rather than leaving that step to the homeowner.

## Why Garretson Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Garretson's setting at the edge of the Sioux quartzite outcrops that form Palisades State Park is part of what makes the town beautiful, and part of what makes it flood-prone. Quartzite doesn't absorb water the way sandstone or soil does, so a heavy spring storm or fast snowmelt sheets off the rock formations around Devil's Gulch and funnels straight toward Split Rock Creek and the lower-lying streets near downtown. Homes close to the creek, especially older properties with block or poured foundations built before modern drain tile standards, tend to take on water through foundation cracks and window wells rather than from a single dramatic breach. That slower, pressure-driven seepage is sneaky: it can soak wall cavities and subfloor for days before carpet or drywall show visible staining.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Garretson

We start every flood call with a moisture map, using meters to find out how far water has traveled into framing, insulation, and subfloor, not just what's visible at the surface. From there the process is extraction, then controlled drying with air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the structure, then antimicrobial treatment on any materials that stayed wet long enough to risk mold growth. In Garretson's older homes, especially in the Downtown Garretson area where many houses still have original lath-and-plaster or early drywall, we pay close attention to baseboards and wall cavities because moisture wicks upward slower than it does in newer construction, which means a wall can test dry at knee height and still be saturated near the floor plate. We document every step with photos and moisture readings for your insurance file as we go, not after the fact.

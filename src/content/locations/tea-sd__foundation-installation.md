**Foundation Installation in Tea?** New construction here looks nothing like it did a decade ago. Subdivisions like Sunrise Ridge and Prairie Meadows are going up on ground that was cropland two summers ago, and the clay-heavy soils under that cropland behave very differently once a foundation and a finished grade sit on top of them. Getting the footing depth, drainage, and curing schedule right the first time matters more in a town growing this fast than almost anywhere else in the region.

## Why Tea's Growth Is Changing Foundation Work

Tea's expansion as a bedroom community southwest of Sioux Falls means a lot of foundation installation happening on lots that were platted and graded within the last few years, not decades-settled ground. Freshly moved fill soil compacts unevenly if it isn't tested and tamped correctly, and southeastern South Dakota's frost line runs deep enough that footings need to sit well below grade or they risk heaving when the ground freezes and thaws. We've seen the difference between a slab poured on properly compacted fill near Downtown Tea's older lots versus a newly subdivided parcel out past Legacy Park, where the soil profile is still settling. Both need different handling, and treating them the same is how cracks and uneven slabs show up two winters later.

## Our Foundation Installation Process in Tea

We start with a soil and site assessment before any concrete gets ordered, since Lincoln County's clay content varies block to block even within a single development. From there: excavation to the required footing depth, compaction testing of the base, forming, rebar placement per the engineered plan, and a pour schedule that accounts for South Dakota's swing between hot, dry summer days and the first hard frost, both of which affect cure time. We also grade for drainage away from the structure before backfilling, which matters more in newer Tea subdivisions where the surrounding lots haven't fully settled and runoff patterns are still shifting as neighbors finish their own yards and driveways.

## Tea Permitting & HOA Coordination

Foundation permits for work in Tea typically run through Lincoln County, and we handle the application and inspection scheduling as part of the job so homeowners aren't tracking down separate approvals mid-build. A good number of the newer developments around Sunrise Ridge and Prairie Meadows also carry HOA architectural review requirements that touch foundation height, setback distance, and sometimes exterior finish at grade level. We pull HOA guidelines early in the process rather than after excavation starts, since a foundation height adjustment after the footings are poured is a far more expensive fix than catching it on paper. ## Local note

One thing that trips up a lot of new Tea homeowners: lots near the edges of town, closer to the older agricultural drainage ditches, can carry more residual moisture in the subsoil than lots nearer Tea Area High School and the more established grid. We test moisture content at the footing trench before we pour, not just at the surface, because a foundation that looks dry on top can still be sitting on saturated clay a foot down, especially in spring after snowmelt works through the fields that used to drain that water somewhere else entirely.

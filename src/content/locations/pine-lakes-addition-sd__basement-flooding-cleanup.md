## Why Pine Lakes Addition Basements Flood

Much of the basement flooding we see in this part of South Dakota traces back to the clay-heavy soil that's typical across the region. Clay expands when it's saturated and contracts hard when it dries out, and that cycle works against poured concrete and block foundations over the years, opening hairline cracks that widen with each freeze-thaw swing. Pine Lakes Addition's lower-lying lots and proximity to area lakes mean the water table sits closer to the surface than it does in the higher ground around Sioux Falls, so a sump pump that's merely adequate most of the year can get overwhelmed fast during a heavy spring thaw or a slow-moving summer storm. Homes built with a single sump pump and no battery backup are the ones that call us most often after a power outage knocks the pump offline right when it's needed.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Pine Lakes Addition

We start by pumping and extracting standing water, then map moisture in the walls, subfloor, and framing with meters so we know exactly how far the water traveled, not just where it's visible. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers and dehumidifiers are placed and monitored daily, with adjustments based on how the specific materials in your basement, finished drywall, exposed concrete, carpet pad, are releasing moisture. If the water came from a sewer backup or sump failure rather than clean groundwater, we treat and sanitize affected materials before drying equipment goes in, since contaminated water changes what can be saved and what has to come out. Our technicians carry IICRC training into every job, which matters most on the calls where the source of the water isn't obvious until the drywall comes off.

## Why Anderson Properties See Storm Damage

South Dakota sits in a corridor that gets hit hard by spring and summer severe weather: fast-forming thunderstorm cells, large hail, and straight-line or tornadic wind events that can flatten a shelterbelt or peel back a roof section in minutes. Anderson's mix of older farmhouses and newer rural construction means roofing materials and attic ventilation vary a lot from property to property, which changes how fast wind-driven rain finds its way into insulation and ceiling drywall. Many properties here also rely on shelterbelts, the rows of trees planted generations ago as windbreaks, and when a storm takes down mature trees in those rows, the debris often lands on outbuildings, fence lines, or the home itself rather than open ground.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Anderson

The first visit is about stopping further loss: tarping exposed roof sections, boarding broken windows, and removing fallen limbs that are still resting against siding or gutters. From there we move to water extraction if rain got into the structure, using moisture meters to check whether water tracked into wall cavities or just the surface. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying, with daily moisture checks rather than guesswork, because a roof that looks tarped and dry on day one can still have saturated sheathing underneath. Once the structure is stable, we scope the rebuild, whether that's a few courses of shingles or a full section of roof deck and drywall.

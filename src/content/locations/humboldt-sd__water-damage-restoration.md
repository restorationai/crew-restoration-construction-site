## Why Humboldt Properties See Water Damage Issues

Eastern South Dakota's clay-heavy soil doesn't drain the way sandier soil does. During spring thaw and after heavy summer storms, that clay holds water against foundation walls longer than homeowners expect, which puts steady pressure on basement walls and slab joints in Humboldt's older housing stock. Add in a hard freeze-thaw cycle every winter, and pipes that were fine in December can crack by February once ground movement shifts a foundation just enough to stress a joint. Many Humboldt homes still run original galvanized or early-generation PVC supply lines, and those are the lines we see fail most often, usually at a fitting rather than mid-pipe.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Humboldt

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out of carpet, flooring, and basement slabs before it has a chance to wick further into drywall and framing. From there we map moisture with meters, not guesswork, checking subfloor and wall cavities that can read dry on the surface while staying saturated underneath. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and adjusted based on daily moisture readings rather than a fixed schedule. For homes with finished basements, a common build type in Humboldt, we pull baseboards and drill small weep holes in drywall when needed so trapped water behind the wall can actually dry instead of sitting there colonizing mold over the following week.

## Local note

A lot of Humboldt basements were finished out years after the house was originally built, which means the vapor barrier behind the drywall is often inconsistent or missing in sections. When we dry a finished basement here, we test moisture at multiple depths rather than assuming the whole wall behaves the same way, because one stud bay might dry in two days while the one next to it, missing a barrier, holds water for a week longer.

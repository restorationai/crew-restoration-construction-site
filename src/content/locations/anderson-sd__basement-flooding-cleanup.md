## Why Anderson Basements Flood

Much of the flooding we see in this part of eastern South Dakota traces back to the ground itself. Heavy clay soil doesn't drain the way sandier soil does, so when spring snowmelt or a hard summer thunderstorm saturates the yard, water has nowhere to go but toward the path of least resistance, which is often a foundation wall with hairline cracks or a window well that's lost its seal. Older Anderson homes with block foundations are especially prone to this because mortar joints shift slightly every winter as the ground freezes and heaves, opening tiny gaps that widen year over year. Sump pumps help, but a single power outage during a storm, which isn't uncommon on rural lines feeding properties outside town, is often all it takes for a basement to take on several inches of water overnight.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process

We start by identifying the source, because a cracked foundation wall, a failed sump pump, and a sewer backup all call for different handling, and mixing up the approach wastes time you don't have. Once the source is addressed or at least controlled, extraction comes first: submersible pumps for standing water, followed by truck-mount extraction for carpet pad and flooring that's still holding moisture. From there we pull baseboards and, where needed, the lower section of drywall to expose wall cavities, since insulation behind drywall holds water long after the surface looks dry. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and monitored daily until moisture readings in the studs and subfloor confirm the space is actually dry, not just dry to the touch.

## Local note

A good number of basements in and around Anderson were finished decades after the home was originally built, which means the drywall, baseboards, and flooring in the lower level are often a different generation of material than what's upstairs, and sometimes a different installation standard entirely. That matters during cleanup because older finish work in these basements was frequently installed without a vapor barrier behind it, so once drywall gets wet, moisture travels sideways into adjoining stud bays faster than it would in a newer build. We check moisture spread further than the visibly wet section for exactly this reason.

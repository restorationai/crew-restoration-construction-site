A roof replacement in Pine Lakes Addition means working around South Dakota's swing between hailstorms, 60 mph wind gusts, and winters that drop well below freezing, sometimes all within the same calendar year. That cycle is hard on asphalt shingles and harder on homeowners trying to figure out whether a repair will hold or whether it's time for a full tear-off. Crew Restoration & Construction has been installing and replacing roofs around the Sioux Falls area since 2015, and we size every job to what this region's weather actually does to a roof deck, not a generic shingle warranty chart.

## Why Pine Lakes Addition Roofs Wear Differently

Platted additions like this one tend to go up in waves, meaning a lot of homes in the area share the same roofing age, the same builder-grade shingle, and the same failure point. When one roof in a block starts shedding granules or losing tabs after a hailstorm, it's common for several neighboring roofs to be close behind, even if only one household has noticed the damage yet. South Dakota's freeze-thaw pattern compounds this: moisture that works into a cracked shingle or compromised flashing in November can expand and tear the material wider by February, well before any visible leak shows up inside the attic.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process

We start with a full roof inspection, not just a shingle count, looking at decking condition, flashing around chimneys and vents, and whether the existing ventilation is adequate for the attic space. For replacements, that means pulling the old roofing down to the deck, checking for soft spots or rot that a surface-level quote would miss, and installing synthetic underlayment rated for wind-driven rain rather than older felt paper that tears more easily during a South Dakota spring storm. Ice-and-water shield goes along eaves and valleys, since ice damming is a real risk here any time snow sits on a warm attic and refreezes at the roof edge. We close out with proper ridge and soffit ventilation, which matters as much for shingle lifespan as the shingle brand itself.

## Insurance and HOA Coordination for Roofing Projects

Many homes in platted additions carry recorded covenants that govern roof color, shingle style, or material class, even decades after the original builder is gone. We check those covenant requirements before ordering materials so a replacement doesn't get flagged after installation, which is a slower and more expensive problem to fix than confirming it upfront. On the insurance side, hail and wind events are the most common trigger for a roof claim in this part of South Dakota, and we document the damage with dated photos and a written scope before any tear-off begins, which gives homeowners a clear record to bring to their adjuster.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in older sections of platted additions: roofs installed in the same construction wave often have the original builder's ventilation shortcuts baked in, undersized ridge vents or boxed-in soffits that never moved enough air. Replacing shingles without correcting that ventilation just buys the same problem back in ten or fifteen years, so we treat attic airflow as part of the replacement scope rather than an upsell.

## Why Salem Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Salem sits on flat prairie ground with heavy clay soil that holds water instead of letting it percolate away, so a hard spring rain or a fast snowmelt off farm fields outside town can push groundwater against foundation walls for days after the storm has passed. Downtown Salem's older housing stock, much of it built with brick or block foundations and shallow basements, tends to take on water through hairline cracks and old mortar joints rather than a single dramatic breach. Properties near the McCook County Fairgrounds area sit a little lower and are the first to show standing water in yards and window wells when county ditches can't keep up. Homes closer to Lake Vermillion face a second risk: rising water table during wet years, which shows up as damp slab edges even without a visible storm event.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Salem

We start with extraction and moisture mapping to find out how far water has traveled behind walls and under flooring, because in a lot of Salem's older homes the visible wet line on a basement wall is only part of the story. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed according to the actual moisture readings rather than a standard room count. Pre-1960s plaster and block construction common downtown releases moisture slower than the drywall and engineered flooring in Salem's newer subdivisions, so we adjust drying timelines and equipment placement by house age rather than applying one schedule to every job. Once materials read dry, we document everything for the insurance file before any rebuild work starts.

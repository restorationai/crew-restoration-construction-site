## Why Anderson Properties See Water Damage Issues

This part of South Dakota runs on a continental climate: hard freezes through winter, a fast spring thaw, and clay-heavy soil that doesn't drain quickly once saturated. That combination is rough on residential plumbing and foundations. Frost heave can shift a foundation just enough to crack a slab or stress a joint that's been fine for decades, and when the ground thaws in March and April, saturated soil pushes groundwater toward any low point, including basement walls and crawl spaces common in older farmhouse and ranch-style homes throughout the area. Add an unheated or poorly insulated outbuilding, and a single cold snap can split a supply line overnight.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Anderson

We start with moisture mapping, using meters to find out how far water has traveled behind drywall, under flooring, or into a basement slab, not just where it's visibly pooling. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water out with truck-mount or portable units depending on how much access the property allows. From there we set air movers and dehumidifiers and monitor drying progress daily, following the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing at when a space is actually dry. In rural and semi-rural properties around Anderson, we also check crawl spaces and outbuildings that a standard inspection might skip, since those areas often hold moisture the longest and are where secondary problems start.

Lead paint abatement in Madison almost always starts with a question nobody wants to ask: how old is this paint, really? A lot of the housing stock around Downtown Madison and near Dakota State University predates 1978, which means layers of paint on trim, sills, and porch railings may carry lead that's been painted over again and again. Before any remodel, sale, or tenant turnover, that question needs a real answer, not a guess.

## Why Madison Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Madison's older neighborhoods grew up around the rail line and the lake, and a good share of that construction, especially the modest frame houses near the university and the cottages that ring the Lake Herman area, went up well before lead was pulled from residential paint. South Dakota's freeze-thaw cycle is hard on exterior paint film: moisture gets under old layers in the fall, freezes, and pops chips loose by spring. That chipping and chalking is exactly how lead dust ends up in window wells, garden soil, and on porch floors where kids and pets spend time. Homes that have been through a few DIY paint jobs over the decades are often worse off than untouched originals, because sanding without containment just redistributes the problem.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Madison

Work on any pre-1978 property falls under EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting Rule, which sets the containment, cleaning, and verification standards we follow on every job regardless of city. In practice that means sealing off the work area with plastic sheeting, using HEPA-filtered vacuums and wet-sanding methods instead of dry sanding or open-flame removal, and running a post-job cleaning verification before anyone moves furniture back in. On Madison jobs we also factor in the building: balloon-framed older homes near Prairie Village often have paint buildup inside wall cavities at window and door jambs, not just on visible trim, so testing goes beyond the obvious surfaces. We test first, document findings, then scope the actual abatement or encapsulation work so homeowners aren't paying to remove paint that tested clean.

## Local note

A fair number of the cabins and year-round homes near Lake Herman were built as lake retreats decades before anyone worried about paint composition, then converted to full-time residences later.

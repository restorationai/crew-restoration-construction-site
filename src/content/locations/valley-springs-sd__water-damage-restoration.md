## Why Valley Springs Properties See Water Damage Issues

Valley Springs sits in the southeastern corner of South Dakota, close enough to the Minnesota line that winters bring the same deep freeze-thaw cycles common across the region. Frozen ground pushes against foundation walls and buried supply lines, and when a hard freeze breaks, water finds the weakest seam, whether that's a cracked basement wall, a split hose bib, or an aging water heater that finally gives out after a cold snap. Many properties here are rural or semi-rural, built on well and septic systems rather than municipal utilities, which changes how we approach shutoff and drainage during an active loss. Heavy clay soils common to this part of the state also drain slowly, so a surface flood or a failed sump pump can sit against a foundation far longer than it would in sandier ground, pushing moisture through block walls over days instead of hours.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Valley Springs

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on how far a property sits from road access. From there we move to structural drying calibrated to what's actually wet, not just what's visible. A lot of Valley Springs homes have exposed basement framing or older paneling over block, and both hold moisture differently than modern drywall assemblies. We take moisture readings through the drying process and follow IICRC S500 guidelines for drying verification rather than guessing at timelines, because pulling equipment too early on a block foundation almost always means a callback for hidden dampness or early mold growth.

## Why Brandon Properties See Foundation Challenges

Much of Brandon, including the newer construction around Aspen Heights and the Brandon Valley area, sits on former farmland with heavy clay content. Clay-rich soil expands when it absorbs spring moisture and contracts in dry months, which puts uneven pressure on footings that aren't set deep enough or reinforced correctly. South Dakota's frost depth also matters here: footings in this part of the state typically need to sit well below the frost line, often cited around 42 inches locally, so a shallow pour that might work in a milder climate simply isn't an option. Lots near the Split Rock area, closer to Split Rock Creek, also tend to hold more seasonal moisture, which factors into how we grade and waterproof below grade.

## Our Foundation Installation Process in Brandon

We start with a soil assessment specific to the lot, since clay content and drainage can vary even within the same subdivision. From there we excavate to the required depth, form the footings, place rebar per the engineered plan, and pour. Because of the clay and frost considerations common in this part of Minnehaha County, we pay close attention to footing depth, drain tile placement, and damp-proofing or waterproofing membranes before backfill. For walkout basements, which are common on the rolling lots near Brandon Golf Course, grading and exterior drainage get extra attention so water moves away from the foundation instead of pooling against it.

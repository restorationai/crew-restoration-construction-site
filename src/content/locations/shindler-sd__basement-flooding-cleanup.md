## Why Shindler Basements Flood

Much of southeastern South Dakota sits on dense clay subsoil that doesn't drain quickly. When heavy spring rain or fast snowmelt saturates the ground around a foundation, hydrostatic pressure builds against basement walls and slab seams faster than a sump pump can keep up, especially in older homes where the system is undersized for today's storm patterns. Freeze-thaw cycles through a Dakota winter also stress foundation walls over time, opening hairline cracks that let groundwater seep in long before anyone notices a wet spot on the carpet. Homes built before municipal storm sewer upgrades are particularly prone to backup during heavy rain events, since older clay or cast-iron lines can't always handle the volume modern development adds upstream.

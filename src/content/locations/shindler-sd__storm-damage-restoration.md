## Why Shindler Properties See Storm Damage

Eastern South Dakota sits in a corridor where spring and summer bring fast-forming thunderstorm cells, hail, and the occasional tornado touchdown. Shindler's mix of older farmhouse-style construction and newer ranch-style builds means roofing materials, flashing details, and attic ventilation vary a lot from property to property, and that variation changes how a storm actually gets inside a house. A roof that shed water fine for thirty years can fail at a single lifted shingle edge once wind gets underneath it, and once water is tracking down into the attic insulation, the clock on secondary damage, sagging drywall, and eventual mold growth starts immediately. Tree damage is its own category here too. Mature cottonwoods and elms common on older lots can take out a section of roof or fence line in one gust, and a downed limb resting on a roof often hides a puncture that isn't obvious from the ground.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Shindler

We start with a roof and exterior assessment, documenting wind, hail, and tree impact points with photos carriers can use directly. If the structure has an active breach, we tarp or board it the same visit to stop ongoing water entry, then move to interior extraction and drying if rain has already gotten into ceilings, walls, or flooring. Our IICRC-trained team dries structures to the S500 standard rather than guessing at "looks dry," which matters in homes with plaster or older lath construction that hold moisture longer than modern drywall. Once drying is confirmed with moisture meters rather than a visual check, we move into reconstruction, matching roofing, siding, or interior finishes so the repair doesn't stand out against the rest of the house. Tree removal and debris hauling get coordinated alongside the structural work so you're not managing two separate crews.

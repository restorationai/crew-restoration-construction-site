## Why Renner Corner Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of the land around Renner Corner is flat, low-gradient prairie, which slows natural runoff during spring thaw and summer storm bursts. That standing water has to go somewhere, and on properties without curb-and-gutter infrastructure, it often finds a basement window well, a foundation crack, or a sump pit that wasn't sized for the volume. South Dakota's freeze-thaw cycle adds another layer: pipes run through unheated crawlspaces or exterior walls in older farmhouses can crack during a hard freeze and not leak until a thaw days later, so the damage shows up well after the cold snap that caused it. Homes on private well and septic systems, common in this unincorporated part of the county, also change how we evaluate a loss, since a sewage backup or well pressure tank failure behaves differently than a municipal water main break.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Renner Corner

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount or portable pumps depending on how the property is accessed, then move into moisture mapping with penetrating meters to find where water has traveled under flooring and into wall cavities. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, and in this area that often means adjusting for slab-on-grade construction in newer builds versus raised foundations and crawlspaces in older farmhouses, since each dries at a different rate and needs different air movement. We document moisture readings daily so you and your adjuster have a clear record of when materials reached dry standard, not just when the visible water was gone.

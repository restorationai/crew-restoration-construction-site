## Why Dakota Dunes Properties See Flood Damage

The combination of sandy river-bottom soil and a shallow water table means drainage around foundations doesn't always behave the way it would on higher ground. Sump pumps work harder here, and when levee-protected areas do take on water, it tends to arrive fast and recede slowly, leaving saturated fill soil pressing against foundations for days. Homes in The Dunes golf community often have extensive landscaping and irrigation systems close to the structure, which can mask a slow foundation leak until it's already soaked into baseboards and wicked up drywall.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Dakota Dunes

We start with moisture mapping, checking framing, subfloor, and wall cavities with meters rather than guessing from surface appearance, since drywall can look dry while the stud behind it is still wet. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water and saturated carpet pad before it migrates further into the structure. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and monitored daily until materials read dry, not just damp to the touch. On walkout basements, which are common throughout this community, we pay particular attention to the wall-floor joint, since that's where river-driven groundwater and interior flooding most often meet.

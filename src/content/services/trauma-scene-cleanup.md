An unattended death, violent incident, or traumatic accident leaves behind more than what's visible on the surface. Blood and bodily fluids carry bloodborne pathogens that can soak into subflooring, grout lines, and wall cavities within hours, and the longer the scene sits, the more porous materials absorb. This is not a job for household cleaners or a well-meaning family member with a mop. It requires trained technicians, regulated disposal, and a process built around both health standards and the privacy of the people involved.

## What Trauma Scene Cleanup actually involves

Trauma scene cleanup covers the decontamination of spaces affected by death, violent injury, or accidents involving blood and bodily fluids, including suicides, homicides, unattended deaths, and serious accidental injuries. The work goes beyond visible residue. Our technicians assess which materials can be cleaned and restored and which need to be removed entirely, such as saturated carpet, padding, or drywall. Equipment includes EPA-registered disinfectants, enzymatic cleaners formulated to break down organic material, and in some cases ozone or hydroxyl machines to address lingering odor. We coordinate entry with property owners, law enforcement, or medical examiner staff as needed, since many scenes remain under investigation before cleanup can begin. Most residential trauma cleanups are completed in a single visit; larger or more extensive scenes may take longer depending on the materials affected.

## Our process

1. **Coordination and access.** We confirm the scene has been released by authorities and walk through what the family or property manager needs before work starts.
2. **PPE and containment staging.** Technicians suit up in protective equipment and seal off the work area to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected parts of the property.
3. **Removal of affected materials.** Porous materials that cannot be fully decontaminated, like carpet, padding, or sections of drywall, are removed and bagged according to biohazard waste protocols.
4. **Decontamination and odor treatment.** Remaining surfaces are cleaned with enzymatic and EPA-registered disinfectants, followed by odor neutralization where needed.
5. **Final inspection and disposal documentation.** We verify the space meets cleanliness standards and provide documentation of proper biohazard waste disposal for records and insurance.

## What separates a good trauma scene cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake in trauma cleanup is treating it like a standard deep clean. Surface disinfecting without removing saturated subfloor or drywall leaves contaminated material behind, which can create odor and health issues weeks later. A thorough technician checks under baseboards, inside wall cavities near the point of impact, and beneath flooring, not just what's visible on top. Waste disposal is another area where shortcuts show: biohazard material has to be bagged, labeled, and manifested for transport to a licensed facility, not bundled with regular trash. Insurance adjusters reviewing a claim typically want photo documentation of affected areas, an itemized list of removed materials, and proof of proper disposal, so thorough recordkeeping from the start matters as much as the cleaning itself.

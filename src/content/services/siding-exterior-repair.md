Siding that's cracked, buckled, or missing panels isn't just a cosmetic problem. Once a gap opens behind a panel or a seam pulls loose from house wrap, wind-driven rain gets behind the cladding and starts wetting sheathing that was never designed to dry out on its own. In Sioux Falls, where hail and straight-line wind events can strip siding off an entire elevation in one storm, a small dent or crease left unaddressed over a winter often turns into a sheathing repair by spring. This page covers how we evaluate and repair siding and exterior damage across the Sioux Empire.

## What Siding and Exterior Repair actually involves

Siding repair ranges from replacing a handful of damaged panels to re-cladding an entire elevation after hail or wind damage. The work depends on what's underneath: house wrap and sheathing condition, whether the original siding is vinyl, fiber cement, steel, or wood lap, and how the damage occurred. A hail strike that cracks vinyl is a different repair than a wind event that tears panels off their nailing strip, and both are different from long-term moisture damage where siding has been hiding rot in the sheathing for months. On most jobs we're matching existing panel profile and color as closely as the manufacturer's current line allows, checking flashing at windows and corners, and confirming the water-resistive barrier underneath is intact before anything new goes back on. Straightforward panel replacement on a single elevation can often be completed in a day; full-house re-siding or jobs involving sheathing repair run longer and are scoped before work starts.

## Our process

1. **Exterior inspection.** We walk the full exterior, not just the damaged section, checking for hail bruising, cracked caulk joints, loose panels, and signs of moisture intrusion at seams, corners, and penetrations like hose bibs and vents.
2. **Sheathing and moisture check.** Where siding has been compromised for a while, we check the sheathing underneath for soft spots or elevated moisture readings before deciding whether the repair is cosmetic or structural.
3. **Scope and material match.** We identify the existing siding profile, color, and manufacturer where possible, and document everything with photos for insurance review before ordering material.
4. **Removal and repair.** Damaged panels, trim, and any compromised house wrap or sheathing come off. We repair or replace the barrier underneath before new siding goes on, because covering a wet wall with new panels doesn't solve anything.
5. **Install and seal.** New siding is installed to manufacturer spec with proper nailing, expansion gaps, and flashing details, then seams and transitions are sealed against the next storm.

## What separates a good siding repair from a bad one

The most common shortcut we see is siding reinstalled over wet or damaged sheathing because it's faster and cheaper up front. That repair looks fine for a season and then fails from the inside out. A thorough repair always checks what's behind the siding, not just the panel itself. Nail pattern matters too: siding nailed too tight restricts the panel's normal expansion and contraction and causes buckling later, especially during Sioux Falls' temperature swings between summer heat and winter cold. Color and profile match is another place corners get cut; siding product lines get discontinued, and a repair using a close-but-not-quite match is obvious from the street and can affect resale value. Insurance adjusters reviewing storm claims generally want documentation of hail impact spacing and density across the whole elevation, not just the worst-looking panels, since isolated patch repairs on a storm-damaged elevation often don't hold up to later inspection.

Costs vary widely depending on siding material, how much of the exterior is affected, and whether sheathing repair is involved. ## Seasonal & regional considerations

Hail season across the Sioux Empire tends to peak in late spring and summer, and those storms are the most common reason we're called out for siding damage. Winter brings its own stress: repeated freeze-thaw cycles can work loose caulking and seams that were already compromised, letting moisture in behind panels during the coldest months when it's hardest to spot. Getting storm-damaged siding assessed before winter sets in, rather than after, generally means a simpler repair.

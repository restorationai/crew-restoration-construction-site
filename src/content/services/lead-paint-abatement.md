Lead paint abatement comes up for one of a few reasons: a pre-1978 home is about to be renovated, a child's blood lead test came back elevated, a real estate transaction is stalled on a lead disclosure, or paint on windowsills and door trim is visibly chalking and chipping. Sioux Falls has a meaningful stock of homes built before the 1978 federal ban on residential lead paint, particularly in the older neighborhoods near downtown and along the river corridor, which means disturbing a wall, sanding a sill, or pulling old trim can release lead dust long before anyone notices a problem.

## What Lead Paint Abatement actually involves

Abatement is different from simply painting over old layers. It means permanently eliminating a lead hazard, whether that is removing painted components entirely, encapsulating surfaces with a specialized sealant rated for lead containment, or replacing building elements like windows, doors, and trim that can't be made safe otherwise. Work starts with lead paint testing, either XRF surface readings or lab-analyzed paint chip samples, so the scope is based on actual lead content rather than the age of the house alone. Once a hazard is confirmed, the work area gets sealed off with poly sheeting and negative air containment to keep dust from migrating into living space or the HVAC system. Depending on the number of components and square footage involved, a single-room abatement project typically runs a few days; a whole-house scope involving multiple rooms, exterior siding, or a full window replacement runs longer.

## Our process

1. **Lead paint testing** - surface testing and, where needed, paint chip sampling to confirm which components contain lead and at what concentration, so the scope of work is based on data instead of assumption.
2. **Containment setup** - isolating the work area with sealed poly barriers, negative air pressure, and HEPA filtration to keep lead dust from spreading to adjacent rooms or outside the structure.
3. **Lead-safe removal or encapsulation** - using wet methods, HEPA-attached sanders, and specified encapsulants to either remove painted components or seal them permanently, following lead-safe renovation work practices rather than dry sanding or open-flame burning, which are prohibited for this exact reason.
4. **HEPA cleanup and clearance** - HEPA vacuuming of all surfaces, wet wiping, and a visual clearance check before containment comes down, confirming no paint chips or dust residue remain in the work area.
5. **Waste handling and documentation** - lead-contaminated debris is bagged and disposed of according to state waste regulations, with documentation provided for your records or for a real estate closing.

## What separates a good lead paint abatement response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating lead paint like a cosmetic problem: scraping, sanding, or demoing painted trim without containment because the paint "doesn't look that bad." Dry sanding or dry scraping on lead paint is one of the fastest ways to contaminate a room with dust that settles into carpet, HVAC returns, and window tracks for months. A second issue is skipping testing altogether and assuming a 1960s house is automatically lead-free because a previous owner repainted. Paint layers compound: a newer coat can sit directly over the original lead-based layer, and disturbing it releases both. A careful operator tests before touching anything, documents lead levels in writing, and uses wet methods and HEPA-filtered tools throughout. Insurance adjusters and real estate inspectors reviewing this kind of work look for clearance documentation, waste manifests, and a clear paper trail showing lead-safe work practices were followed, not just a verbal assurance that the job is done.

## What does Lead Paint Abatement cost?

Costs vary widely depending on how many components are involved, whether the approach is encapsulation or full removal, and whether windows or exterior siding need replacement rather than just interior trim. Every property is different, which is why Crew Restoration & Construction provides a written scope of work after testing, before any abatement work begins. Homeowners insurance typically does not cover lead paint abatement as a standalone project unless it is tied to a covered loss, such as fire or water damage that requires reconstruction of components that also happen to contain lead.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Sioux Falls winters push most interior abatement work indoors with sealed containment regardless of season, but exterior siding and trim projects are best scheduled in the spring through fall window when temperatures allow encapsulants and paints to cure properly. Homes going through real estate transactions often need testing and remediation completed on a tight closing timeline, which is worth flagging early so testing and scope development aren't the bottleneck.
